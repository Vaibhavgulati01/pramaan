## Sources actually used

| Purpose | Dataset | Licence | How it is accessed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real product photos (legit class, catalog photos) | [Amazon Berkeley Objects](https://registry.opendata.aws/amazon-berkeley-objects/), via the `amaye15/amazon_berkeley_objects` HF mirror | **CC BY-NC 4.0** (non-commercial, attribution required) | Streamed at build time, cached under `data/raw/` |
| AI-generated images (synthetic-fraud class), 8 generator families | `TheKernel01/Tiny-GenImage`, derived from [GenImage](https://github.com/GenImage-Dataset/GenImage) | **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** | Parquet row-group reads over `hf://` (byte-range, never a full-shard download), cached under `data/raw/` |

**No dataset images are redistributed by this repository.** `data/` is
gitignored in full. What is committed is the *recipe*: `manifest.json`
records, per claim, the upstream dataset, its licence, the source image's
SHA256, the transform applied, and the output SHA256 — enough for a third
party to rebuild the corpus byte-identically from the original sources.
This is the "ship the builder, not the images" requirement in
`PRAMAAN_v2_architecture.md` §5, and it is also what keeps a public
Apache-2.0 repo clear of NC-licensed image redistribution.

Generator families map to the spec's holdout protocol as
`train {SD15, BigGAN}` · `calibrate {GLIDE}` · `test {Midjourney, ADM,
VQDM, Wukong}` (`benchmarks/simulate_ledger.py: GENERATOR_HOLDOUT`).

**One declared substitution.** The spec (§6) names **SD 1.4** as a train
family. The public mirror we source from declares an `SD14` label in its
parquet schema but contains **zero SD14 rows** — verified by reading the
`generator` column across shards 0–2, which return only
`{Real, ADM, BigGAN, GLIDE, Midjourney, SD15, VQDM, Wukong}`. We
substitute **SD 1.5** rather than drop the family, which preserves the
holdout structure exactly (two train, one calibration, four test, all
seven disjoint). This is recorded here, in the `GENERATOR_HOLDOUT`
definition, and in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md` rather than silently swapped.

## The simulated component — declaration 2 of 3

*(The other two: the README's Data section, and the module docstring at
the top of `benchmarks/simulate_ledger.py`.)*

There is no public dataset of refund claims with claimant history, so
**every claimant identity, order record, claim timestamp, and device
fingerprint in PRAMAAN-Bench-v1 is synthetic**, generated by
`benchmarks/simulate_ledger.py` with parameters anchored to published
rates where the spec cites one (~15% fraud among returns; ~23% India
RTO). Only the *images* are real, sourced data.

Consequences, stated plainly:

- Pillar 4 (claimant behaviour) is trained and evaluated entirely on
  simulated data. Its measured contribution is **not** evidence about
  real claimant behaviour.
- Headline results are therefore reported from the real image pillars
  first, with the behavioural pillar's contribution reported separately.
- `docs/REAL_DATA_ONRAMP.md` specifies the exact schema a merchant would
  supply to replace the simulator.
- A sensitivity sweep over the least-certain simulator parameters lands
  in Phase 6.

## How a claim's image is constructed

Two stages, and the separation matters:

1. **Fraud transform** — what the fraudster did (`recycle_transform`,
   `screen_rephotograph_transform`,
   `metadata_inconsistent_edit_transform`). Absent for
   `legit_real_photo` and for `fraud_catalog_photo`, whose fraud is
   contextual rather than a pixel edit.
2. **Transport** (`apply_transport`) — how the image reached the
   merchant: resize into a fixed band, re-encode, and strip metadata on
   the messaging-app route (~70%) or preserve it on the direct-upload
   route (~30%). Applied to **every** claim, with parameters drawn
   **independently of its class**.

Stage 2's symmetry is a correctness requirement, not tidiness. §5 asks
for degradation so "the legit class isn't separable by file cleanliness
alone"; applying it to only one class inverts the problem. An early build
did exactly that and left synthetic-fraud claims at 512×512 against
~256×256 for everything else — image size alone nearly separated the
classes. See `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`; guarded by
`test_image_dimensions_carry_no_label_signal`.

A consequence worth stating: because transport is class-independent, the
`fraud_metadata_inconsistent_edit` signal survives only on the
direct-upload route. That is a property of the domain — WhatsApp really
does strip the evidence — not a defect.

## Splits, and why the corpus drops a few claims

The four split constraints (§6: generator-holdout, temporal,
entity-disjoint, ring-disjoint) are enforced in two stages by
`benchmarks/splits.py`:

1. **Reconciliation.** Entity clusters (fuzzy — phone/email/PIN-bucketed
   address) and reuse rings (`image_group_id`) are unioned into
   components; each component is pinned to the split of its earliest
   claim, and members in other splits are **dropped**.
2. **Verification.** All four constraints are then re-checked, and the
   build hard-fails if any still fails.

Stage 1 is necessary because entity resolution is fuzzy and no threshold
makes coincidental merges impossible — two independently generated
claimants can land on genuinely ambiguous addresses (observed: `H.No. 06
Tata Marg, Sri Ganganagar` vs `H.No. 06 Sarraf, Sri Ganganagar`, same
PIN, same house number, different street). Real merchant data has the
same property. Rather than assume the matcher is perfect, the build
enforces the constraint structurally and **reports the drop count** in
`manifest.json` (`reconciliation.n_dropped`); observed loss is well under
0.1%.
